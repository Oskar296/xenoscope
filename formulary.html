XENOSCOPE · Advanced mode · synthesis bench

Field Formulary

Every cure, and how to build it from raw materials. In Advanced mode you don't pick a finished drug — you make one: a raw material plus a preparation step. This card is how you know which.

The one rule

The material decides which drug you get. The step is the correct method to make it. Then just match the drug to what you diagnosed — run the assays first so you know the target.

🧫

Ferment

Grow a microbe (a mould or soil bacteria) so it secretes its drug.

🌿

Extract

Steep a plant part or tissue in solvent and draw the compound out.

🔥

Heat / react

Drive a reaction — make soap, concentrate brine, denature protein, build the antiviral.

⚗️

Filter / purify

Strain out solids and isolate the pure agent — water, serum, charcoal.

Saving a sick organism

Preserve

You diagnosed the affliction attacking the host. Make its one true cure — the biology is honest, so the wrong drug does nothing and hurts the patient.

VirusDiagnosis · Virus
Spot it: a bare protein capsid packed with foreign nucleic acid — and no ribosomes of its own.
Nucleoside stock+🔥 Heat / react→Antiviral
Why: a nucleoside analogue is a counterfeit genome building‑block. When the virus copies itself it swallows the fake and the chain dead‑ends. Nothing else touches a virus.
BacteriumDiagnosis · Bacterium
Spot it: a peptidoglycan wall (Gram stain reacts), its own 70S ribosomes, no nucleus.
Penicillium mould+🧫 Ferment→Antibiotic
also: Soil Streptomyces + Ferment → Antibiotic (streptomycin)
Why: penicillin (from mould) shatters the bacterial wall; streptomycin (from soil bacteria) jams the 70S ribosome. Bacteria only.
FungusDiagnosis · Fungus
Spot it: a chitin wall and branching thread‑like hyphae.
Griseofulvin mould+🧫 Ferment→Antifungal
Why: antifungals hit targets only fungi have — the ergosterol in their membrane or the chitin in their wall. (Griseofulvin, this one, jams the fungus's cell‑division machinery.)
ParasiteDiagnosis · Parasite
Spot it: a motile, nucleated eukaryotic cell — it shrugs off antibiotics.
Sweet wormwood+🌿 Extract→Antiparasitic
also: Cinchona bark + Extract (quinine) · Soil Streptomyces + Extract (ivermectin)
Why: artemisinin, quinine and ivermectin poison a eukaryotic parasite's metabolism while sparing the host's cells.
PrionDiagnosis · Prion
Spot it: the nucleic‑acid assay finds nothing at all — it's pure misfolded protein, not alive.
Urea / acid+🔥 Heat / react→Denaturant
Why: there is no organism to kill. A prion is a shape — only a chaotrope + heat that unfolds the protein can destroy it. Antibiotics/antivirals are useless.
ToxinDiagnosis · Chemical toxin
Spot it: no invader at all — the cells are dying around a diffusing poison. Every organism assay comes back empty.
Immune serum+⚗️ Filter→Antitoxin
also: Activated charcoal + Filter → Antitoxin (adsorbs the poison)
Why: nothing to kill — you neutralise a molecule. Purified antibodies bind it; activated charcoal soaks it up. A living‑pathogen cure won't work.

Destroying an invader

Neutralize

No infection here — the organism is the threat. Identify its kingdom from the assays, then exploit the one structural weakness of its cell. The right weapon depends entirely on its wall (or lack of one).

BacteriumKingdom · Monera
Tell: Gram stain + nuclear stain — a prokaryote with a peptidoglycan wall.
Penicillium mould+🧫 Ferment→Antibiotic
or: Egg white + Extract → Lysozyme (cracks the same wall)
Why: the peptidoglycan wall + 70S ribosome are exactly what an antibiotic attacks; lysozyme is an enzyme that dissolves that wall directly.
ArchaeonKingdom · Archaea
Tell: run the membrane‑lipid assay — ether‑linked lipids. It's the only way to separate it from a bacterium.
Plant oil+Lye+🔥 Heat / react→Detergent (soap)
Why: archaea have no peptidoglycan wall for an antibiotic to attack — the classic trap. With no wall to target, you go after the membrane instead: soap (oil + lye, boiled — real saponification) breaks open the lipid bilayer.
FungusKingdom · Fungi
Tell: wall analysis returns chitin.
Griseofulvin mould+🧫 Ferment→Antifungal
Why: a chitin‑walled cell falls to the antifungal — a drug aimed at fungus‑only targets (the ergosterol membrane or the chitin wall) that the host doesn't share.
PlantKingdom · Plantae
Tell: a rigid cellulose wall + chloroplasts (pigment scan).
Sea salt+🔥 Heat / react→Herbicide (brine)
Why: the stiff cellulose wall resists bursting, so flooding it with water won't work. Boil salt to a hypertonic brine and draw water out — the cell collapses (plasmolysis).
AnimalKingdom · Animalia
Tell: wall analysis + nuclear stain — a wall‑less cell with a nucleus.
Distilled water+⚗️ Filter→Osmotic shock
Why: no wall to hold it together. A hypotonic (pure‑water) shock floods the cell until it swells and bursts.
ProtistKingdom · Protista
Tell: a single‑celled eukaryote with no wall.
Distilled water+⚗️ Filter→Osmotic shock
Why: like an animal cell, it has no wall — the same pure‑water osmotic burst does it in.

When the case fights back

Two invaders (co‑infection)

The morphology assay shows two different attackers. Make both cures and administer each — one alone only gets you halfway.

Biofilm shield

A slime layer blocks every drug. Make Soap (Plant oil + Lye → Heat) first to strip the film, then apply the real cure to the exposed cells.

Preserve ≠ Neutralize

Curing an infection targets the invader; neutralizing an organism targets its own cell's weakness. Same bench, completely different logic — always read the orders.
